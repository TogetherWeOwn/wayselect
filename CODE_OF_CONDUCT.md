# Code of Conduct

Wayselect is a small, review-driven project: every change lands as one
reviewable slice with green checks on the exact head that merges
([`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md)). That only works if everyone in the
room — maintainer, contributor, first-time reporter — is treated with respect.
This document sets that expectation and what happens when it is not met.

Adapted from the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/).

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in Wayselect a harassment-free experience for
everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Our standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

- Using welcoming and inclusive language, and being respectful of differing
  viewpoints and experiences.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback tied to the work —
  the diff, the fixture, the reason code — not the person.
- Focusing on what is best for the project and its community; owning mistakes
  and learning from them.
- Showing empathy toward other community members.

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

- The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances
  of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks.
- Public or private harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption.
- Publishing other people's private information (addresses, emails, access
  details) without explicit permission.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, reviews,
discussions, and any other repo-hosted venue — and when an individual is
officially representing the project in public.

## Reporting

**Do not open a public issue, PR, or discussion with a conduct report.**
Contact a maintainer through a private channel and share details only after
they acknowledge — the same private-first rule
[`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) uses for vulnerability reports. (Suspected
vulnerabilities themselves go through the `SECURITY.md` process, not this
one.) Reports are handled confidentially: only the reporter and the handling
maintainers see the details.

A good report includes what happened, where (links to comments, commits, or
threads), who was involved, and when. Screenshots or quotes help; do not
re-post private details publicly to make your point.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying standards, moderating project
spaces, and taking appropriate, fair corrective action in response to any
behavior they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Consequences scale with impact and history: a private warning and request to
correct course; temporary or permanent removal from project spaces (issues,
PRs, reviews) for repeated or serious violations; and, for the most serious
cases, a permanent ban plus removal of contributions where the platform
allows. Maintainers communicate decisions that affect a participant directly
to that participant; broader disclosures stay minimal and factual.

## No retaliation

Retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith, or who cooperates with
a review of a report, is itself a violation. Reports made in good faith that
turn out to be mistaken carry no penalty — but deliberately false reports do.
